# 05 - Google Ads Transparency Center + YouTube (Shorts)

Research date: 2026-10-04. Region filter: United States. Source: adstransparency.google.com (read-only, no login), plus YouTube oEmbed lookups for video titles.
Owner note: simple English. "Ad" = paid. "Organic" = free posts.

---

## 1) TL;DR (5 lines)

1. **No coloring-book publisher advertises big on Google.** Bobbie Goods, Coco Wyo and Usborne have **0 ads** in the US. Coloring-book sellers win on Amazon and with free social video, not Google ads.
2. **The kids-book brands that DO pay Google are "gift" and "learning" brands**: Wonderbly and Hooray Heroes (personalized story books, ~400-900 ads each), Highlights (~300), ABCmouse (~900), Lovevery (~300), Lakeshore and Hand2Mind (~200 each). Most of their ads are **Search text ads**. Video is only 5-25% of what they run.
3. **The hooks that win are about the parent, not the kid**: "The Perfect Baby Gift", "Personalized Grandpa Gifts", "Hello Magazine for Ages 0-2", "#1 Learning App for Kids 2-8", "Get school-ready". They name an age, a benefit (reading, school-ready) or a gift moment.
4. **Crayola's YouTube ads are "how-to make" craft videos** ("How To Make A Buzzing Bee", "How To Make A Clay Dog"), run by an agency for its "Crayola Learning" channel. The lesson for us: the video shows the art being made, then the brand.
5. **For us:** stay organic first. Make Shorts that show a page being colored (timelapse) with big text naming the age + the moment ("Bedtime book for 3-year-olds", "Christmas gift under $10"). Set Shorts to **NOT made for kids** and talk to parents. Later, a small paid test should be a Search ad on gift words, or a YouTube in-feed/Shorts ad using our best organic Short.

## 4) Winning formats ranked (with evidence)

1. **Gift-moment message (Search text + short video)**. Evidence: Wonderbly ~900 ads and Hooray ~400 ads still running. "Perfect Baby Gift", "Grandpa Gifts". Ad money keeps flowing only when it makes money. -> Use for Nico (Christmas), and later for birthdays.
2. **Age + benefit headline**. Evidence: Highlights "Ages 0-2", ABCmouse "Kids 2-8", "school-ready". -> Always say "Ages 3-6" and one benefit (first reading words, calm bedtime, screen-free).
3. **Show the process, then the result (how-to / timelapse)**. Evidence: Crayola Learning's paid "How To Make..." videos. -> Coloring timelapse of one page, ending on the full colored page next to the book cover.
4. **Proof / trust line**. Evidence: "#1", "millions of young readers", "developed by experts". -> We can't say "#1". We can say true things: "Big bold lines for little hands", "1 big picture + 1 easy sentence per page", star rating once we have reviews.
5. **Theme that kids already love**. Evidence: Monster Jam ads from the Melissa & Doug agency. -> Rocco the monster truck will fit this demand.

## 6) YouTube Shorts rules that matter for us

(From general YouTube policy knowledge. I did not re-check the official pages this session - please confirm.)

- **Made for kids (COPPA)**: If a video is set "made for kids", YouTube turns off **comments**, the **notification bell**, **personalized ads**, **end screens/cards**, saving to playlists and the mini-player. That hurts reach and makes clicks to Amazon nearly impossible. Our plan is **"not made for kids"**, so:
  - Speak to **parents**: "for your 3-6 year old", "bedtime routine idea", "gift idea".
  - Avoid kid-bait: no nursery-rhyme style songs aimed at kids, no "Hey kids!", no cartoon characters talking to children. Coloring art is fine. The key is **who the video talks to**.
  - YouTube can override your setting if the video clearly looks made for kids. Keep a parent-facing voiceover/text so it stays "not for kids".
- **Links**: Links in Shorts descriptions and comments are **not clickable**. Use: (1) the channel **About/links** section with the Amazon link, (2) "Amazon search: Benny the Bear Cozy Day" as text on screen, (3) a **Related video** link from the Short to a longer video (a flip-through) whose description has a clickable Amazon link.
- **Length**: Shorts can be up to 3 minutes, but for traffic keep it **15-45 s**. The hook must land in the first 1-2 seconds.
- **Titles**: Put the keyword parents search ("coloring book for toddlers", "bedtime story", "Christmas gift for kids"). Add #shorts and 1-2 niche hashtags.
- **Music**: Use YouTube Audio Library / Shorts sounds only (copyright).
- **Paid later**: Google Ads cannot target users under 18. Ads are shown to adults (parents), which matches our buyer. Do **not** set the promoted video as "made for kids" (made-for-kids videos have limited ad options).

## 7) Five concrete YouTube Shorts / Google ad concepts (no filming of kids or hands)

**Concept 1 - "Sleepy Bear Timelapse"**
- Hook (0-2s): finished, colorful Benny page fades to black-and-white. Text: "Bedtime coloring for ages 3-6".
- Format: digital coloring timelapse (screen recording in Procreate/Ibis Paint style app), then page with the big sentence, then the book cover. Calm music.
- Length: 25-35 s.
- Title: "Coloring a sleepy bear for bedtime (ages 3-6) #shorts"
- Description: "Benny the Bear's Cozy Day: one big picture + one easy sentence per page, for kids 3-6. Find it on Amazon: search 'Benny the Bear Cozy Day'. Link on our channel page."

**Concept 2 - "Color it, then read it" (the unique part)**
- Hook: Text "This coloring book is also a story book". Show page 1 black-and-white, voiceover reads the sentence slowly, page fills with color, next page.
- Format: slideshow + voiceover (AI or own voice, adult tone talking to parents).
- Length: 30-45 s (4-5 pages).
- Title: "A coloring book your 4-year-old can READ"
- Description: "Big bold pictures + big-letter sentences. Kids color and practice first words. Ages 3-6. Search 'Little Crayon Tales' on Amazon."

**Concept 3 - "Christmas gift under $10" (Nico)**
- Hook: Text "Stocking stuffer for ages 3-6 (under $10)" over a finished snowy reindeer page.
- Format: 3 fast flip-through pages (digital mockup of the open book), then cover + price. Gift-style, like Wonderbly's "perfect gift" angle.
- Length: 15-20 s.
- Title: "Screen-free Christmas gift for kids 3-6 🎄" (one emoji ok)
- Description: "Nico the Little Reindeer's Snowy Day - color & read Christmas story. Search 'Nico the Little Reindeer' on Amazon." Also good as a later **Search ad**: headline "Christmas Coloring Book Ages 3-6" / "Color & Read Story - Big Easy Pages".

**Concept 4 - "Guess the color" (parent-and-child prompt)**
- Hook: Text "Ask your kid: what color should Benny's blanket be?" Show black-and-white page, pause 2 s, then color it 3 different ways.
- Format: 3 quick color variations (digital), end with cover.
- Length: 15-25 s.
- Title: "What color would YOUR kid pick? (coloring idea)"
- Description: parent-facing prompt + book name. This drives comments (only possible when NOT made for kids).

**Concept 5 - "How to color a reindeer in 3 steps" (Crayola-Learning style how-to)**
- Hook: Text "Easy reindeer coloring for little hands - 3 steps".
- Format: step 1 base colors, step 2 details, step 3 background snow (digital). Ends with "Full story book: Nico the Little Reindeer".
- Length: 30-40 s.
- Title: "How to color an easy reindeer (ages 3-6)"
- Description: tips for parents ("let them pick colors, big crayons work best") + book name. This mirrors the "How To Make A..." videos Crayola pays to promote.

Bonus for Rocco (later): "Monster truck coloring - VROOM in 20 seconds". Monster Jam advertises a lot, so boys' monster-truck demand is real.

## 8) What to avoid

- **Don't spend on Google ads first.** The big coloring-book brands (Bobbie Goods, Coco Wyo) run 0 Google ads. Prove a video works organically, then pay to boost it.
- **Don't set Shorts "made for kids"** and don't make videos that talk to kids. You lose comments, end screens and links.
- **Don't promise what we can't prove** ("#1", "teacher approved", "expert designed") - ads get rejected and it breaks trust.
- **Don't put the Amazon link only in the Short description** (it isn't clickable). Use the channel link + on-screen text.
- **Don't use copyrighted music or characters** (no Disney, Bluey, Paw Patrol in titles or tags).
- **Don't show the AI-made look** without a check: the pictures must be clean bold lines; blurry or broken lines in a close-up look cheap.
- **Don't run generic Search keywords** like "coloring book" (Amazon, Walmart and big brands win those). Use long, specific ones: "christmas coloring book for 4 year old", "bedtime coloring book toddler".
